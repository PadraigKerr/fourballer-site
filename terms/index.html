1. What Fourballer is

Fourballer helps golf groups organise rounds, keep score, apply handicaps and work out each player's result for the games the group chooses to play. Fourballer only keeps records. It never holds, sends or receives money. It sets no values: any amount attached to a game is agreed by the players themselves. And it takes no part in any arrangement between players. "Mark as settled" records that a group considers a round square; it doesn't move money.

6. Scores and results: a record of your inputs, not an official scorecard

Fourballer is a note-taking tool. Everything it shows (scores, handicap strokes, game results, standings and who owes whom) is worked out only from what players type in and the settings the group chooses. It records your play; it doesn't watch it. We don't monitor, check or verify what players enter, and it's your responsibility to judge whether the scores and results are accurate and complete.

  • It isn't an official scorecard. Keep a traditional scorecard, or another record your group trusts, for every round. Where the app and your scorecard disagree, your scorecard and your group's agreement decide, not the app.
  • Check it together before you settle. Players should compare scores hole by hole and agree the results between themselves before anyone marks the round as settled. Once it's marked settled, its results are final in the app.
  • Record your scores as the Rules of Handicapping require. Enter each score honestly and in line with the USGA Rules of Handicapping and your own responsibilities as a player under them. That duty stays with you when you use Fourballer.
  • Anyone in a round can enter or change scores. When two phones disagree, the change that reaches us last is the one kept. Every change is recorded, but the app can't tell which entry is right.
  • Errors can happen. A wrong input, a setting chosen by mistake, a lost connection or a fault in the app can all produce a wrong result. We work hard to get the calculations right, but we don't promise they're free of errors, and we aren't responsible for decisions or arrangements players make based on them.
